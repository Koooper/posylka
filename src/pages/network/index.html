Inside the network

Four things that make this story bigger than a delivery company.

From following refugees to a multinational group

When millions of Ukrainians fled in 2022, they asked for the service they knew from home. The company followed. Co-founder Volodymyr Popereshniuk described the goal as following Ukrainians to Europe to remain their support abroad.

Within two months of opening in Poland, daily parcels between Poland and Ukraine went from about 100 to 1,500. Former employees who had fled asked to work in the new branches.

Today its European network has 995 service points across 16 countries (mid-2026). On average about 60% of its European customers are Ukrainian; in Moldova it is under 1%, which shows it can win local customers too. It reported profits in four European countries in early 2026.

Two borders tightening at once. From July 2026 the EU charges a €3 customs duty on low-value imports, with a handling fee due by November 2026. Ukraine voted in October 2026 to charge VAT on cheap foreign online purchases, applying no earlier than July 2027. Cross-border shopping is about to get more expensive in both directions.

The morning everything stopped

At 08:03 on 26 April 2025, a power fault hit a single Ukrainian cloud data centre, De Novo. For 14 minutes its batteries and generators were blocked. Nova Post’s app went down. So did Дія (Diia), the government’s app for digital ID and public services, and the state bank Oschadbank. In Kyiv, contactless payment at metro turnstiles failed. It was not a cyberattack. Nova Post was fully back by 11:38.

That morning showed something usually invisible: a private parcel company had become part of the country’s basic digital plumbing, alongside the state and the banks.

It is not officially designated national infrastructure. A fair description is “economically critical private infrastructure”.

Four rails that run through a branch

Moving things. Parcels, pallets, tyres, generators. About 24 hours between the country’s most distant cities, the company says.

Moving money. Its payment arm, NovaPay, handles about 22.7% of all domestic money transfers in Ukraine, according to the central bank. Pay-on-delivery lets strangers trade online without trusting each other in advance.

Plugging into shops. Online stores connect to Nova Post’s software so that labels and tracking happen automatically. When that connection fails, small sellers can’t ship.

Plugging into the state. Soldiers use the Ministry of Defence’s Army+ app to generate barcodes that let them send or receive a parcel for one hryvnia at frontline branches.

A second front, online

  • 2022The IT director said attacks on company servers had risen five- to six-fold since the invasion, and about a fifth of the IT budget now went on security.
  • Dec 2023A cyberattack that Ukrainian officials blamed on Russian military hackers knocked out Kyivstar, the biggest mobile operator. About 24 million people lost signal. Nova Post lockers kept opening via Bluetooth for people who had loaded the app beforehand.
  • Dec 2024Fake emails claimed people owed Nova Post money. Impersonating a trusted brand is easier than hacking it.
  • Apr 2025The data-centre power fault (see “Rails of the online economy”). An outage is not always an attack.
  • Oct 2026A DDoS attack (flooding servers with fake traffic) disrupted the app, accounts and the system shops use to create labels. Backup procedures kicked in; services returned the same day. No data compromise was reported, and no attacker was named.

Careful readers notice what is not known: who was behind the 2026 attacks, or whether they were timed with missile strikes. Don’t claim more than the evidence shows.

The war, by the numbers

Company-reported unless noted. Dates matter.

Read numbers like a reporter

Most figures about Nova Post come from the company itself. That doesn’t make them false, but it means you should say “the company reports”.

Watch the denominator. The company said shipments grew 10% in 2025. Its own published 2024 total (480 million) gives 8.75%; another reported total (486 million) gives about 7.4%. All three numbers are “real”; they just don’t agree.

Separate people carefully. Some of those killed were company staff, some were drivers for partner companies, and some were employees who had joined the army. Adding them into one number hides who bears which risk.

Plans aren’t outcomes. “Will build 600 shelters” and “built 600 shelters” are different sentences.
